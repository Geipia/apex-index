About the ranking

How ERAZ points are calculated from published results, and where the data comes from.

How points are calculated

1

Normalization

For each benchmark, the score is scaled to 100: (score − floor) / (best − floor). The floor is the random-guess level (25% for GPQA, 10% for MMMU-Pro, 1000 Elo for GDPval, 0 otherwise). The best tracked model gets 100.

2

Categories

The 14 benchmarks are grouped into 7 categories. Within each, the average is weighted: the most discriminating tests (HLE, FrontierMath Tier 4, FrontierSWE, GDPval) weigh 1.5×, ARC-AGI-3 1.25×, saturated tests (GPQA) 0.5×, the others 1×.

3

Overall score

Weighted average of the categories where the model has results. Default profile: Reasoning, Math, Code, Agents ×1; Knowledge ×0.75; Long context and Multimodal ×0.5.

4

Coverage

A model that only publishes its best results should not win. Final points equal score × (0.75 + 0.25 × coverage) × 10, i.e. 1000 points at most.

Why not SWE-bench Verified, MMLU or AIME? These tests are saturated (over 95% for the best models) or contaminated: they no longer separate frontier models. The Artificial Analysis index is shown for reference but excluded from the calculation, since it already aggregates several of the benchmarks used here.
